The measurement layer

Systems Measurement

Most organizational measurement tools ask people how they experience work. That information has value. It locates reported strain. It does not, by itself, identify the organizational mechanisms beneath it.

Reported experienceEngagement, morale, and confidence
Operating mechanismsOwnership, authority, handoffs, routing, process load, and performance discipline

Monderman calls that second layer Systems Measurement. It examines ownership, authority, handoffs, approvals, escalation, routing, process load, and performance discipline. It establishes a baseline that can be measured again after an organization acts.

The distinction is supported by published evidence. Global engagement rose over the longer trend, then fell in 2024 and again in 2025. Controlled interventions produced small average increases in self-reported engagement. Research on management practices found strong associations with firm performance, and one randomized trial raised productivity by 17 percent in the first year. These studies did not test Monderman's instruments.

Sentiment measurement shows where people experience strain. Systems Measurement examines the organizational systems beneath that experience.

The measurement architecture

One path from context to a saved baseline.

Structured answers determine the score. In Interview mode, AI may phrase questions and code free-text replies into the instrument's existing structured answer options; the scoring engine itself is deterministic. AI may also assist with post-score interpretation, but it does not calculate or set the score.

Repeatability means version discipline. Within the same instrument/scorer version, the same structured answers produce the same score. Like-for-like longitudinal comparison requires compatible versions.
