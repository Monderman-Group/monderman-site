Your evaluation plan

Five steps to a useful evaluation.

  1. Choose a real organizational question.

    Identify a problem your organization needs to understand, such as delayed approvals, unclear responsibilities, or repeated administrative work. Name the organizational scope you will examine, including the teams, departments, divisions, or workflows involved. Agree who will review the findings and decide what to do next.

  2. Invite participants to the first relevant diagnostic.

    A Workspace admin opens Measure, creates a Diagnostic campaign, and sends participant links. Start with six to twelve people who know the same scope, including different roles where useful. Choose a run length they can complete. Review the individual reports before drawing a group conclusion.

  3. Add other diagnostics when they answer another question.

    All four are available. You do not need to use every diagnostic, depth, or participant perspective to complete the evaluation. Select the ones that add relevant evidence:

    • Structural Clarity: examine ownership, authority, role boundaries, and handoffs.
    • Decision Velocity: examine where approvals, reviews, and escalation slow decisions.
    • Operational Systems: examine the time and effort required by processes, reporting, and coordination.
    • Institutional Performance: examine reliable delivery and the ability to respond to change.

    Include the people who know the work, across the roles and units in your chosen scope. One person can contribute to several diagnostics. Campaign readiness is checked separately for each lens; completing more lenses does not count one person as several participants.

  4. Use Synthesis to examine the submitted evidence.

    Use Depth Synthesis for eligible runs from the same Diagnostic to examine the median score, spread, coverage, and differences between participant perspectives. Compare results across teams, departments, or divisions where the inputs are compatible. Use Cross-Lens Synthesis for eligible results from different Diagnostics to examine their relationship while keeping each measure distinct. Eligibility depends on the inputs and their compatibility; a Composite Score is withheld when the evidence does not support one.

  5. Choose a practical action and a later measurement.

    Decide what deserves investigation or change. Have a Workspace admin record an action and an owner in Action Plans, and agree a review date with the people responsible. Keep the original results as a baseline, then repeat a compatible measurement when enough time has passed for the change to matter.

    Use the 60 days to collect evidence, compare findings, plan an improvement and decide whether to continue. The Workspace shows your remaining days. Continuing on a paid plan requires a separate purchase; the evaluation never charges or renews automatically.
